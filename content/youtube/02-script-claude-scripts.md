# Script — "How to Use Claude to Write YouTube Scripts in Under 10 Minutes"

- **Tone:** confident, conversational, slightly edgy
- **Runtime:** ~8–9 min
- **Structure:** `[HOOK]` → `[BODY]` → `[CTA]`

---

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:30]**

Most YouTubers spend four hours writing one script. I just wrote three this morning. Before my coffee got cold.

[PAUSE]

And no — this isn't "type a prompt, paste the garbage, post it." That stuff gets you 200 views and a dislike ratio.

[VISUAL: split screen — left "4 HOURS 😩", right "9 MINUTES 🔥"]

There's a specific way to drive Claude that turns it from a fancy autocomplete into an actual writers' room. Get one part wrong and it sounds like a robot read a press release. I'll show you the exact part most people skip — but first, the trap.

[VISUAL: zoom into eyes, text overlay "THE TRAP"]

**[BODY]**

Here's the mistake. You open the AI, you type *"write me a YouTube script about X,"* and you hit enter.

[PAUSE]

And what comes back is technically a script. It's also unwatchable. Flat. No hook. Sentences a human would never say out loud.

That's not the AI's fault. That's a *briefing* problem.

[VISUAL: crumpled paper labeled "write me a script" hitting a trash can]

Think about it. If you hired a real writer and your whole brief was five words — you'd get garbage too. Claude is the same. It's only as sharp as the constraints you hand it.

[PAUSE]

So here's the system. Three moves.

**Move one — give it a role and a structure.** Don't ask for "a script." Tell it: *you're a retention-focused YouTube writer, use this exact structure — hook, body with pause markers, soft CTA.* The second you hand over a skeleton, the quality jumps. Hard.

[VISUAL: screen recording — typing the role + structure prompt]

[PAUSE]

**Move two — feed it your voice.** This is the part everyone skips. Paste in a transcript of a video you already love — yours, or someone whose style you'd kill for. Tell Claude: *match this rhythm. Short sentences. Contractions. A little edge.*

Now it's not writing like the internet average. It's writing like *you.*

[VISUAL: highlighting a pasted transcript, arrow pointing to "← your voice"]

[PAUSE]

**Move three — and this is the one that separates pros from prompt-monkeys — you edit out loud.** Don't read the draft. *Perform* it. Any line that trips your tongue, you cut or you ask Claude to rewrite punchier.

[VISUAL: person reading script aloud, red pen striking lines]

Ten minutes, tops. Because you're not writing anymore. You're directing.

[PAUSE]

And remember that "payoff" I teased? The part most people skip? It's move two. The voice transcript. Skip it and you get a robot. Add it and people swear you wrote every word yourself.

[VISUAL: text overlay "VOICE = THE WHOLE GAME"]

**[CTA — soft]**

That's the whole workflow. Role, voice, perform.

If this saved you a few hours this week, you might like the free prompt pack I keep in the description — it's the exact template I started this video with. No pressure. It's just there if you want it.

Either way — go write something. You've got no excuse now.

[VISUAL: end card, subtle subscribe nudge bottom-right]
