## Video 1 — "Stop Overpaying Tax as a Freelancer (Set This Up Once)"

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:30]**

Every year, millions of freelancers tip the government. By accident. They overpay, underpay-then-panic, or get hit with a penalty they never saw coming.

[PAUSE]

It's not because tax is hard. It's because nobody gave them the *system*. You set this up one time, and tax season goes from a heart attack to a non-event. Let me show you the setup — including the percentage almost everyone gets wrong.

[VISUAL: text overlay — "SET UP ONCE. NEVER PANIC AGAIN."]

**[BODY]**

Here's the core problem. When you're employed, tax comes out *before* you ever see the money. As a freelancer, the full amount hits your account — and your brain treats all of it like it's yours.

[PAUSE]

It isn't. A chunk of every payment belongs to the tax office. You're just holding it.

[VISUAL: a payment landing, then a slice peeling off labeled "NOT YOURS"]

So system step one: the moment a client pays you, move a fixed percentage into a separate "tax" account. Out of sight. You can't spend what you can't see.

[PAUSE]

What percentage? This is the part people get wrong — they guess 10%, then get wrecked. For most freelancers, setting aside **25 to 30 percent** is the safe lane until you know your exact rate. Too much beats too little — a refund is a happy surprise, a tax bill you can't pay is a nightmare.

[VISUAL: 30% slice moving into a "TAX" account]

Step two: quarterly. In a lot of countries, freelancers are expected to pay tax four times a year, not once. Miss those, and you get penalties on top of the bill. Put four dates in your calendar right now. That's it. That's the whole defense.

[PAUSE]

Step three: track as you go, not in April. One simple sheet — money in, expenses out. Because every legit business expense lowers what you owe. We'll do a whole video on the deductions people miss.

[VISUAL: simple income/expense sheet filling in]

Do these three things — separate account, right percentage, quarterly dates — and you've beaten 90% of freelancers who wing it.

## Video 2 — "The Freelancer Money System: Where Every Dollar Should Go"

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:30]**

You can have a great month and still feel broke. Big invoice clears, and somehow it's gone in two weeks. Sound familiar?

[PAUSE]

That's not an income problem. That's a *plumbing* problem. One bank account, and every dollar — tax money, rent money, profit — gets mixed into one blurry pile you slowly drain. Let's fix the plumbing.

[VISUAL: one messy account, money swirling and vanishing]

**[BODY]**

Here's the idea: money needs *jobs*. When every dollar lands in one account, none of them have a job, so they all default to the same job — getting spent.

[PAUSE]

So we split the river into channels. When a client pays, the money flows automatically into separate buckets, each with one purpose.

[VISUAL: one inflow splitting into four labeled streams]

Bucket one — **Tax.** We covered this. A fixed percentage off the top, untouchable.

Bucket two — **Operating.** This is your "salary" account. The money you actually live on. We'll set the amount in the next video.

[PAUSE]

Bucket three — **Buffer.** The freelancer's secret weapon. A few months of expenses parked here so a slow month doesn't become a crisis. This is what kills feast-or-famine.

Bucket four — **Profit.** Yes, profit — even as a one-person business. A small slice that's *yours*, proof the business works. It funds the bigger goals.

[VISUAL: four accounts, each filling at a different rate]

The magic isn't the buckets. It's that it's **automatic.** You decide the percentages once, set the transfers, and the system sorts every payment without you thinking about it. Discipline you don't have to feel.

## Video 3 — "How to Price Freelance Work So You Stop Undercharging"

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:30]**

If no client has ever pushed back on your price, I've got bad news. You're too cheap.

[PAUSE]

Most freelancers price by anxiety — they pick a number that feels safe and hope it's enough. Then they're overworked and underpaid and can't figure out why. Let's find your real number. The one you've been scared to say out loud.

[VISUAL: text overlay — "YOUR RATE IS PROBABLY TOO LOW."]

**[BODY]**

First, kill the biggest myth: that your rate is your salary. It isn't. As a freelancer, your rate has to cover way more than your time.

[PAUSE]

It covers your taxes. Your unpaid hours — admin, emails, chasing invoices. Your gaps between clients. Your own equipment, software, sick days, the retirement nobody's funding for you.

[VISUAL: a single hourly number, then hidden costs stacking under it]

So if you copy an employee's hourly wage, you're losing money on every job. You need to charge *more* per hour just to break even with a salaried version of yourself. That's not greed. That's math.

[PAUSE]

Now the mindset flip that actually raises your rates: stop selling **time**, start selling **outcomes.** A client doesn't want five hours of your work. They want the result those five hours create. Price the result, and the hourly math stops mattering.

[VISUAL: "5 hours" crossed out, replaced with "the result"]

And here's the test. When you state your price and *nobody* ever flinches, raise it. A healthy rate means roughly one in five prospects says it's too much. If everyone says yes instantly, you left money on the table — every single time.

[PAUSE]

Last thing: raise rates on new clients first. You don't have to have an awkward conversation with everyone tomorrow. Just quote the new, correct number to the next person who asks. Then the next. It compounds fast.
